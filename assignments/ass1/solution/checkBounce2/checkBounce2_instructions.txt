CHECKBOUNCE2: INSTRUCTIONS AND RESULTS SHEET
============================================

Purpose
~~~~~~~
A second, more detailed bounce test, after the results of checkBounce.
It answers three questions:

  1. Press count. In checkBounce, every I2C row showed 20 presses together
     with up to 4 short gaps. Every bounce adds an extra press edge
     (press, release, press), so 20 presses with short gaps should be
     impossible. This test counts presses and releases separately, keeps a
     debounced count next to the raw one, and logs the time of every edge,
     so we can see exactly what happened.
  2. IR polarity. The course plan says PA7 is active-low (LOW = obstacle);
     your sanity check showed the opposite. This test shows PA7 on LD2.
  3. I2C speed. The buttons are read over I2C at 100 kHz, about 2500 reads
     per second, so a bounce shorter than about 400 us is never seen. The
     same tests are repeated at 400 kHz (about 4 times more reads).

Two inputs are watched: I2C button 0, and the IR sensor on PA7.
The B1 button is not tested again: checkBounce showed it is clean.

Nothing moves: the program never drives the wheels, the LEDs or the servo.
This time you need PuTTY as well as Live Expressions: the edge log is
printed to PuTTY.

Where you fill something in, it looks like this:  [ ______ ]
Write your value between the brackets. Where a log is asked for, paste the
PuTTY text between the lines marked "paste here".


PART 1: SETUP (once)
====================

1. Hardware
   - Cart with the IO board connected, as for checkBounce.
   - USB cable from the Nucleo to the PC.
   - Power jumper on the Nucleo: E5V (5 V from the cart; cart power on).

2. Open the project in STM32CubeIDE
   - File > Import > General > Existing Projects into Workspace > Next.
   - Select root directory, depending on the PC you work on:
       D:\git_codes\STM32\ass1\solution\checkBounce2
     or
       C:\Users\yohai\git_codes\STM32\ass1\solution\checkBounce2
   - Make sure "checkBounce2" is ticked, and "Copy projects into workspace"
     is NOT ticked. Finish.
   - Do NOT open checkBounce2.ioc and do NOT press Generate Code. The
     project is ready as it is.

3. PuTTY
   - Connect to the ST-LINK COM port: 115200 baud, 8 data bits, no parity,
     1 stop bit (Connection type: Serial).
   - Before you start: Window > Lines of scrollback = 5000. A log of the IR
     sensor can be 500 lines long.
   - To copy the whole PuTTY text: right-click the PuTTY title bar >
     "Copy All to Clipboard". Before each test, clear the window with
     right-click title bar > "Clear Scrollback", so each copy holds one log.

4. Build and start debugging
   - Select the project, press the hammer (build). Expect 0 errors.
     (This project was only syntax-checked, not built, before you got it.
     If the build shows errors or warnings, copy them into Part 4.)
   - Press the bug icon (F11). If asked, choose "STM32 C/C++ Application",
     then OK. The debugger stops at the start of main().
   - Press Resume (F8). PuTTY prints:
       checkBounce2 running. I2C 100 kHz, log source 0. ...

5. Live Expressions
   - Window > Show View > Live Expressions.
   - Add each name below, exactly as written:

     INPUTS (you will write these):
       resetCounts      write 1: all counts and the log go back to zero
       i2cFast          0 = I2C at 100 kHz, 1 = I2C at 400 kHz
       logSource        0 = log I2C button 0, 1 = log the IR sensor
       dumpLog          write 1: the log is printed to PuTTY
       shortGapUs       30000 (30 ms). Leave it.
       debounceMs       20. Leave it.

     OUTPUTS (you will read these):
       loopsPerSec
       i2cKHz
       i2cOk
       i2cErrors
       i2cHeld
       b0Presses
       b0Releases
       b0ShortGaps
       b0MinGapUs
       b0DebPresses
       irPin
       irFalls
       irRises
       irShortGaps
       irMinGapUs
       irDebFalls
       irDebRises
       logCount

   - Changing i2cFast or logSource resets all counts by itself.
   - resetCounts and dumpLog go back to 0 by themselves.
   - While the log is printing (2 to 5 s), the inputs are not read. Do not
     press anything until PuTTY shows "=== END OF LOG ===".

6. Meaning of the outputs
   - b0Presses / b0Releases : raw edges of button 0, no debouncing.
   - b0DebPresses           : presses counted after a 20 ms debounce.
   - irFalls / irRises      : raw edges of PA7 (1 -> 0 and 0 -> 1). They are
                              named by pin level, not by "obstacle", because
                              the polarity is one of the questions.
   - irDebFalls / irDebRises: the same after a 20 ms debounce.
   - ...ShortGaps           : raw edges less than 30 ms after the previous
                              raw edge of the same input.
   - ...MinGapUs            : the shortest gap, in us. 4294967295 = none yet.
   - In the PuTTY log, every line is one raw edge: its number, its time in
     ms since the reset, the gap from the previous edge in us, and the new
     level. "<short" marks a gap under 30 ms.

HOW I WILL READ THE RESULTS (for your information)
==================================================

  - Presses must equal Releases (or one more, if you were still holding at
    the end). A bounce always adds one press AND one release.
  - DebPresses = 20 confirms your count of 20; Presses - DebPresses is then
    the number of extra presses caused by bounce.
  - In the log, a "<short" line right after a PRESS is press bounce; right
    after a release is release bounce. The gaps show how long a bounce is.
  - Test B against Test A: more short gaps at 400 kHz means the 100 kHz bus
    was missing bounces that are shorter than one read.
  - Test C, C1 against C2: chatter while approaching slowly, but not on a
    quick pass, means the sensor flickers only at its switching distance
    (no hysteresis). DebFalls and DebRises near 10 means the 20 ms debounce
    is enough for Part C and Part E.
